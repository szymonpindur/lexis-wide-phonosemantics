What BY and BH mean

Testing thousands of sound–meaning pairings creates many opportunities for chance findings. False discovery rate (FDR) methods adjust for this multiple testing. At the chosen 5% level, their aim is to limit the expected proportion of false discoveries among the reported discoveries, under the method’s assumptions. This is a property of the procedure across repeated studies, not a 95% probability that any particular pairing is genuine.

BY — Benjamini–Yekutieli: our primary criterion. It allows arbitrary dependence between valid test p-values, with a more conservative adjustment. This matters because semantic dimensions and phonological predictors overlap. A pairing is a primary discovery when it is eligible and its global BY-adjusted p-value is ≤ .05.

BH — Benjamini–Hochberg: our exploratory criterion. It is less conservative and can detect more associations, but its FDR guarantee requires independence or suitable positive dependence. BH-only pairings are available in the maps for further investigation; they are not counted as BY-primary discoveries. Both adjustments cover the complete registered family within each language, across all three phonological representations—not separate corrections for individual maps. What one coloured cell means

Each cell represents one phonological indicator paired with one semantic outcome. Colour is the adjusted coefficient in outcome percentile points: red means higher semantic ranks among carriers, blue means lower ranks. The default view shows global BY discoveries at q ≤ .05, with no effect-size cutoff.

White cells do not pass the chosen display criterion; grey cells are ineligible. Click any cell for its estimate, pointwise confidence interval, controls, diagnostics and scored carrier/noncarrier examples. “All estimable” retains every registered predictor column and shows all eligible coefficients.

Controlling larger lexical structures

The control dictionary combines dictionary-derived morphology, Morfessor segmentation patterns, AI supplementation of component/root/affix analyses and recurring word-edge constructions. Every distinct nonconstant carrier pattern from the original and AI designs is preserved. PDS selects the adjustment set for each pairing.

The goal is a demanding test of phonosemantic associations: offer the model a broad range of larger lexical regularities that could account for a sound–meaning pattern, then examine what remains after adjustment. Comparing control designs quantifies the observed changes in coefficients, uncertainty and discoveries.

Look inside a phonological feature

A feature is an “at least one member phoneme” indicator. Its carrier population can be uneven, and some features have exactly the same carrier set as a single phoneme. The feature maps now expose membership and carrier composition, alongside separate phoneme fits.

How to navigate the maps

  1. Start with BY-primary, then choose a Binder display group if desired.
  2. Drag to pan in the fitted view; wheel/two-finger scrolling zooms. “Fit window” restores the overview.
  3. Use Scrollable view for enlarged cells and native trackpad/keyboard navigation. Axis labels and the shared colour scale stay pinned.
  4. Click a feature cell to inspect constituent phonemes and scored word examples. Examples describe observed ranks, not words’ causal or residualized influence.

Bigram maps include the complete saved grid and examples, so their initial download is large. All six maps work offline with the supplied Plotly file. Download the underlying tables and specification.
